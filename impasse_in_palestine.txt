Impasse in
Palestine
By ALFREDW ERNER
IN February, 1942, the Struma,
a small unseaworthy tub, sank
in the Bosporus: 767 men, women
and children, Jewish refugees
from Nazi terror, were drowned.
The Turks had not allowed them
to land in Istanbul because the
British had refused them entry in
Palestine. “If the Struma had
been an enemy ship,” a British
Labonte then charged, "the German,
Italian, or Japanese passengers
would have been interned."
The plight of the innocent victims of Hitlerism deeply stirred
Mr. Hirschmann, an American
business man. A modern “Scarlet
Pimpernel," he decided to go as
a private citizen to Turkey in order
to see what he could do for
refugees hopelessly stranded in
southeastern Europe.
The Intergovernmental Committee
for Refugees that should
have taken care of them was, he
found, "nothing more than a
decorous facade concealing the
inactivity of the thirty-two governments
which had set it up!'
Early in 1943, however, President
Roosevelt established the War
Refugee Board and instructed the
United States representatives
abroad to "take all measures
within the Government's power
to rescue the victims of enemy
persecution who are in imminent danger of death.” When Mr.
Hirschmann finally arrived at
Ankara, in February, 1944, he
was no longer a private citizen,
but a special attaché of the State
Department charged With carrying
out the orders of the War
Refugee Board in Turkey and the
Middle East. ..

In his candid and lucidly written
memoirs the author gives the
inside story of his mission to Ankara,
of his fight against red
tape, inefficiency, indifference,
even hostility among those who
ought to have been the first to
aid him. Fortunately, he was
wholeheartedly backed by John
Pehle, chief of the War Refugee
Board; by Henry Morgenthau Jr., 
and by our Ambassador Steinhardt. Above all, he had been
granted powers as broad as any
envoy ever had had, including the
permission to deal with -the ene ...
my. He succeeded in making the
Rumanians disband the horror
camp of Transnistria and return
the adult inmates to their homes,
while 5.000 refugee children were
assisted in reaching Constanta,
the Rumanian port of embarkation. However, the Turks did not
permit these refugees to use the
safe land route to the Syrian border
until one of the ships, hired
by Mr. Hirschmann and crowded
with Palestine-bound exiles, had been torpedoed by the Germans.
Mr. Hirschmann dealt with the
evasive British, with a Hungarian
Jew who was a Nazi agent
against his will (selling to the
Allies Jewish lives at a stiff
price), With the vacillating Turkish
authorities and other venal
Balkan governments. He praises
the humanity of the late F. D.R.,
but bluntly charges Lord Moyne,
Cabinet member and Deputy Minister
of State in the Middle East,
with callousness. In Turkey he
found inefficiency, corruption and
bias against non-Moslem minorities.
Palestine was entirely different:
"Compared With the semifeudal world that I had left behind
me in Turkey, here In the
most ancient land was the fullest
supply of youthful optimism.
Here, in the land rooted most
deeply in the historical past, were
to be found ideas, vitality and
tranquil confidence in their new
world."
Since not all homeless Jews
can go to Palestine, other cow1-
tries should accept their due
share of refugees, lest the 1urvivors
of Hitlerism .. die a second
death." The author believes,
though, that Jews and Araba can
very well live together in harmony
in the Holy Land.
THIS is apparently also the
opinion of Mr. Ziff. When ••The
Rape of Palestine" was first published
in 1938 it was rejected as
prejudiced and melodramatic. unscientific
and hysterical. In Its
pre5ent form. stripped of 1ome of
the more detailed accounts and of
all of the supporting notes. but
supplied with a new foreword and
epilogue, it has not become what
one might call an unbiased report.
But thanks to the recent
British fumblings in Palestine it
has gained in importance.
After all, who can deny the
Justification of Ziff's charges
that Britain has been guilty of
much double-talk at the expense
of the Jews ever since she took
over the mandate of Palestine a
quarter of a century ago? However.
his anger does not make
Ziff blind to the great assistance
given to Zionism by such Britishers
as Colonel Patterson, who
headed the Jewish Legion in the
first World War, and Lord Wedgwood, who wanted Jewish Palestine
to become the Seventh Dominion.
Yet Ziff is rather inconsistent
when he lauds democratic Arab
cooperation with the Jewish underground,
and, in the next paragraph,
suggests population exchange-
Palestinian Arabs to be
resettled in the sparsely populated
Iraq to make room for Jews---as
a solution of the Arab-Jewish
problem. True, Mr. Ziff stresses
the voluntary character of the
suggested Arab exodus---but will
there be many Arabs who will be
ready to emigrate to an unknown
land, unless under pressure?
Highly controversial also is his
suggestion to grant Palestine citizenship
to the 2,000,000 displaced
Jews “who have proclaimed their
desire to settle there. and who
now consider themselves expatriate Palestinians.''
